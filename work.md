## Research Scientist (Solid-State Dewetting)

- **Institution:** MIT — Carl V. Thompson Research Group, Department of
  Materials Science and Engineering
- **Years:** January 2016 – January 2017

Led the development of a large-scale, atomistic kinetic Monte Carlo
simulation for studying solid-state dewetting phenomena in thin metal
films, extensible to any lattice structure. Transferred the techniques
of big data and data science to develop algorithms that modeled the
interactions of atoms in a lattice. Used in-memory databases to calculate
positions and energies of particles during the dewetting process.
Parallelized the algorithm using Bayesian statistics in order to
efficiently use graphical processing units (GPUs) to perform massively
scalable physical simulations.

## Physics Tutor

- **Institution:** California State University, Fullerton — Department
  of Physics and Astronomy
- **Years:** September 2005 – June 2007

In our department, we had an informal physics tutoring lounge open to
any student enrolled in a course relating to physics. It was maintained
on a voluntary basis by people willing to offer their time and experience
for interested students. During my first class in physics I was
introduced to the lounge. I immediately formed a study group that would
meet and work together with the help of whoever was volunteering at the
time of our meeting. I continued this study group throughout the
remainder of my time in the department and would often come an hour
early and help students currently enrolled in courses I had already
taken. It is here that my passion for teaching was born and grew.
