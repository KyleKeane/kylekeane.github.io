## Physics and quantum information

<details markdown="1">
<summary><h3 id="measurement-reversal-decoherence-suppression">Decoherence suppression by quantum measurement reversal (2010)</h3></summary>

With Alexander Korotkov (UC Riverside), I proposed the first procedure
to protect a single qubit from energy relaxation using weak quantum
measurement and measurement reversal — without additional qubits and
without entanglement. Published as a Rapid Communication; procedures
proposed in this line of work were subsequently verified
experimentally in superconducting-qubit systems. It remains my
most-cited paper.

**Evidence:**

- [Phys. Rev. A 81, 040103(R) (2010) — Rapid Communication](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.81.040103)
- [The full publication record on the CV](/cv.html#publications)

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary><h3 id="simplified-quantum-error-detection">Simplified quantum error detection and correction for superconducting qubits (2012)</h3></summary>

Demonstrated that for qubits dominated by energy relaxation,
repetitive N-qubit codes can be used for quantum error detection but
not correction, and proposed two-qubit algorithms directly realizable
with the phase-qubit technology of the time.

**Evidence:**

- [Phys. Rev. A 86, 012333 (2012)](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.86.012333)

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary><h3 id="phd-quantum-state-protection">PhD: Quantum State Protection and Transfer Using Superconducting Qubits (UC Riverside, 2012)</h3></summary>

Doctoral work with Alexander Korotkov on theoretical protocols for
quantum information processing: weak measurement, uncollapsing,
quantum error detection and correction, decoherence suppression, and
state and process tomography. Supported by the Chancellor's
Distinguished Fellowship (2007), the Molly Adams Scholarship (2009),
and the Gordon E. Hein Scholarship (2011); the ARO/IARPA
multi-qubit-algorithms programme funded the dissertation research.
Research rotations before the dissertation covered frustrated
magnetism (Kirill Shtengel's group) and the biophysics of virus
capsids (Roya Zandi's group).

**Evidence:**

- [The doctoral dissertation on eScholarship](http://escholarship.org/uc/item/8nq7q2hn)
- Conference record: *Modelling of a Flying Microwave Qubit* (APS
  March Meeting 2012, Boston); *Currently realizable quantum error
  detection/correction algorithms for superconducting qubits* (APS
  2011, Dallas); *Decoherence suppression of a solid state qubit by
  uncollapsing* (APS 2010, Portland); poster at Coherence in
  Superconducting Qubits (ARO, 2010); poster at the IARPA Quantum
  Computing Program Review (2009).

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary><h3 id="undergraduate-experimental-physics">Undergraduate experimental physics with three peer-reviewed papers (CSU Fullerton, 2005–2007)</h3></summary>

In Murtadha Khakoo's electron-scattering laboratory I made
high-precision differential cross-section measurements for electron
impact on atoms and molecules, and designed and machined a rotary
table for variable-geometry scattering experiments. The work produced
three peer-reviewed papers. I was selected as commencement speaker
for the College of Natural Sciences and Mathematics (2007); the
address was titled *Look to this Day*. I did not use a computer until
my second year of college.

**Evidence:**

## Accessibility standards

<details markdown="1">
<summary><h3 id="verbal-description-standards">Verbal-description standards for interactive scientific graphics (2013–2014)</h3></summary>

With a DIAGRAM Center (Benetech) grant, I compiled the best practices
for giving blind students pedagogically equivalent access to
interactive scientific graphics. The recommendations — including the
separation of intent from action — contributed to the creation of the
W3C User-Intent Working Group, and the guidelines were adopted by the
University of Colorado's PhET Interactive Simulations team, whose
implementations became reference examples of accessible interactive
science content.

**Evidence:**

- [Interactive Scientific Graphics: Recommended Practices for Verbal Description (DIAGRAM Center, June 2014)](http://diagramcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Interactive_Scientific_Graphics_Submission23june2014.docx)
- [The full standards-work record on the CV](/cv.html#verbal-description-standards-for-interactive-scientific-graphics)
- Talk record: *Standardizing Textual Descriptions of Interactive
  Graphics* (EDUPUB Workshop, Boston, October 2013); panels at ATIA
  and the Joint Mathematics Meeting (January 2014); *Best Practices:
  Interactive Scientific Graphics on the Web* (DIAGRAM Research
  Meeting, June 2014); DIAGRAM Researcher Series webinar (August
  2014); honorarium address *The Role of Collaboration in Driving

## Science and sonification

<details markdown="1">
<summary><h3 id="black-hole-sonification-accomplishment">Black-hole sonification (2021–present)</h3></summary>

With astrophysicist Erin Kara (MIT), I translated X-ray echo
measurements of black-hole accretion disks into audible sound —
computational mapping from light frequency to pitch that makes the
general-relativity effects in the data audible. Released alongside
the echo-mapping research during Black Hole Week 2022 and reported
worldwide; collaborators included Ian Condry (MIT Spatial Sound Lab)
and Michal Dovciak.

**Evidence:**

## Education at MIT

<details markdown="1">
<summary><h3 id="ppat-capstone">Principles and Practices of Assistive Technology — MIT's co-design capstone (2016–2024)</h3></summary>

Taught on the teaching team from 2016 and led as lead faculty
2022–2024 the capstone subject (6.450 / 6.811 / 2.78 / HST.420) in
which student teams work weekly with a community member with a
disability to co-invent technology for a specific task, through
multiple prototype cycles. The subject honours the legacy of
Prof. Seth Teller; student evaluations ran consistently 6.2–6.4 out
of 7.0. Student projects included a clothing colour-and-pattern
recognition phone application for a blind client, hands-free
birdwatching binoculars, a bicycle with sensing and haptic feedback
for a blind rider, a motorized joystick giving a power-wheelchair
user close access to tables, and an easier help-call application for
assisted-living residents.

**Evidence:**

- [The capstone record on the CV](/cv.html#principles-and-practices-of-assistive-technology-mit-capstone)
- [PPAT course archive](http://ppat.mit.edu/) and [PPAT at MIT CSAIL](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/PPAT/)

<summary><h3 id="humanistic-codesign-initiative">The Humanistic Co-Design Initiative and Project Aakaar (2017–2022)</h3></summary>
