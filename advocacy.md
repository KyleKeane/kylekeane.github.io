## Overview

I am a fierce advocate for the full participation of disabled people in
STEM and in culture. The advocacy is structural. I want the systems that
produce researchers, educators, consultants, and artists to admit disabled
people on the same terms as anyone else — not to run separate
disability-flavoured versions of those roles. The full statement of that
value — the participation model, my language, and what I expect from the
people who work with me — is on the
[Full participation](/full-participation.html) page.

Highlights:

- **[Verbal-description standards](/cv.html#verbal-description-standards-for-interactive-scientific-graphics)**
  — the 2014 DIAGRAM Center guidelines, adopted by PhET and feeding the
  W3C's user-intent work.
- **[The Humanistic Co-Design Initiative](/cv.html#humanistic-co-design-initiative)**
  — co-design fellowships across India, Saudi Arabia, and Uruguay that
  put disabled co-designers inside the engineering team.
- **Accessibility engineering at Wolfram Research** — Section 508
  reviews, VPATs, and working access to Mathematica for blind users:
  see the [Wolfram-era roles on the CV](/cv.html#roles).

## How the advocacy works

The work runs through engineering and standards rather than testimony:
accessibility features built into products used by millions; published
standards that other teams adopt; co-design programmes that change who
is in the room when technology gets made; and consulting for technology
companies, universities, museums, and standards bodies — the scope and
past clients are on the [Engage](/upcoming.html) page. I teach the same
approach through MIT's assistive-technology capstone and the
international programmes on the [Teaching](/teaching.html) page.
