I am a researcher, educator, consultant, and artist working in mainstream
science and technology. I run a research programme, write peer-reviewed
papers, build prototypes, supervise PhDs, give keynotes, and make sound and
tactile art. My retinal condition is one biographical fact among many. It is
not the frame for the work, and this page exists because the alternative is
being typecast — which is a poor use of both my time and yours.

If we end up working together, I'd rather we did so on the assumption that I
am operating at the same level as anyone else with my training and track
record. The rest of this page explains what that assumption actually looks
like in practice.

## A participation model of disability

The World Health Organization's International Classification of Functioning,
Disability and Health (ICF) separates three things that people often
collapse into one: **impairment** (a difference in body function or
structure), **activity limitations** (difficulty performing a task), and
**participation restrictions** (problems experienced in life situations).
The point of the framework is that those three are not the same thing, and
that the third one — participation — is the right unit of measurement for
whether someone is functioning fully in their work.

I have an impairment. With the right environment and the right professional
setup, I have no participation restrictions in research, teaching,
consulting, or artistic practice. The standard isn't lowered or special; the
standard is participation. An engagement that produces participation
restrictions is an engagement that has been set up badly — not an
engagement involving someone who needs less of it.

## What I expect from the people who work with me

Engaging me as a speaker, consultant, supervisor, or collaborator means
meeting the same professional standards you would meet for any expert at my
level, plus a host or producer who understands that materials and venues
need to function for the way I read. Materials shared in advance in formats
that screen readers handle properly. Briefings that describe slides and
visuals as a matter of course. A single technical contact for production
setup. None of this is exotic; it is the difference between running a
competent event and running an apologetic one.

The [Engage](/engage.html) page describes the engagement types I take on
and the standards in detail. The short version: come with a plan, or come
with a willingness to build one with me, and we'll do good work.

## Advocacy, on my terms

I am a fierce advocate for the full participation of disabled people in
STEM and in culture. The advocacy is structural. I want the systems that
produce researchers, educators, consultants, and artists to admit disabled
people on the same terms as anyone else — not to run separate
disability-flavoured versions of those roles.

I will talk about disability when it is the subject of the work. I will
also turn down invitations that exist only because I am blind. The
assumption that my best contribution to a panel is a personal account of
impairment misses what I actually do for a living, and signals an event
that has not done its homework. I am happy to point such invitations
toward people whose primary practice is personal advocacy; mine is not.

## Where this comes from

Lived experience, plus formal training, plus twenty years inside university
physics, MIT engineering, Wolfram Research, and the University of Bristol's
assistive-technology programme. The position is informed but not exhausted
by personal biography. I am not asking for trust on the strength of
identity. I am asking for the same evaluation any other senior contributor
would receive in the same conversation.
