My research sits at the intersection of computational science,
human-centered design, cognitive psychology, and accessibility
engineering. I am interested in how people build meaning from
information that arrives through unconventional sensory channels, and
in the design of computational systems that meet them where they
actually are. I take collaborators and PhD students; the
[Engage](/engage.html) page is the right place to start that
conversation.

## Current research at Bristol

My active research at the University of Bristol is on intersensory
perception science, with four main threads:

### Automated production of audio-tactile graphics

Pipeline work to take complex scientific and educational graphics —
charts, diagrams, simulations, scientific images — and produce
audio-and-tactile representations that are pedagogically equivalent
for blind and low-vision learners. Builds on the verbal-description
guidelines I wrote for the
[DIAGRAM Center](http://diagramcenter.org/accessible-dynamic-scientific-graphics.html)
in 2014, which were adopted by the
[PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu/) team at the
University of Colorado and informed the creation of the W3C
User-Intent Working Group.

### Spatial and immersive audio for echolocation training

Perceptually validated spatial audio for training and supporting human
echolocation. Recent work in this area includes the *Architecting
Perceptible Space* spatial-audio system with Alex Thompson at Bristol,
presented as a poster at the Audio Developer Conference (ADC25) in
November 2025. Connected to performance and installation practice via
the [Creative](/creative.html) page.

### Perception-aware AI

AI systems that need to communicate with people through non-visual
channels — spatial reasoning, scene description, multimodal interface
generation. The *Memory & Cognition* paper with Ben-Ami et al. (2025)
on the effect of graphomotor engagement on visual discrimination is a
recent piece of foundational work in this thread.

### Cross-modal interaction design and inclusive HCI

Children's crossmodal metaphors and playful crossmodal interactions —
*Sense-O-Nary* (Roberts-Morgan et al., IDC '24) — and broader work on
how multisensory interfaces should be designed when one or more
sensory channels are not available.

## Earlier research themes

The threads above sit on top of an earlier body of work in
computational and educational research, and before that in physics.
The selected items below are kept here for context; the full
publication record is on the [CV](/cv.html) page.

### Effectiveness of computer programming for STEM learning

In my role as Computational Curriculum Advisor in MIT's Department of
Materials Science and Engineering, I co-developed the *Computational
Thinking Framework* with Dr. Peter Barendse — a curriculum-design
model for activating computational thinking on MITx using Wolfram
technologies. The framework was deployed across a range of MITx
components and informed work on the relationship between coding,
interactive visualisation, and conceptual reflection in online
introductory physics.

### Standards work on accessible scientific graphics

The 2014 *Interactive Scientific Graphics: Recommended Practices for
Verbal Description* report I wrote at Wolfram for the DIAGRAM Center
is the underpinning of the audio-tactile graphics work I'm doing now.
The recommendation to separate intent from action led to the
formation of the W3C User-Intent Working Group; the verbal-description
guidelines are in continued use at PhET and elsewhere.

### Quantum information (PhD)

My doctoral work with Dr. Alexander Korotkov at UC Riverside was on
weak quantum measurement, uncollapsing, quantum error correction and
detection, and decoherence suppression for superconducting qubits.
The decoherence-suppression-by-measurement-reversal procedure
proposed in our 2010 *Phys. Rev. A* rapid communication was
subsequently demonstrated in an optical adaptation by an experimental
group at POSTECH (*Nature Physics*, 2011). The doctoral dissertation
and core publications are listed on the [CV](/cv.html) page.

### Earlier topics

Selected earlier research projects include inverse design of photonic
crystals using an adaptive mesh search algorithm, GPU-parallelised
atomistic kinetic Monte Carlo simulation of solid-state dewetting in
thin metal films (Carl V. Thompson group, MIT), simulation of the
thermodynamic self-assembly of virus capsids (Roya Zandi group, UC
Riverside), and high-precision differential cross-section measurements
for low-energy electron impact on neon, xenon, and small organic
molecules (Murtadha Khakoo group, CSU Fullerton). Publications from
these projects are on the [CV](/cv.html) page.
